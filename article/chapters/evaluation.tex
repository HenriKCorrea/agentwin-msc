\chapter{Evaluation}
\label{chap:evaluation}

This chapter defines the cross-domain use case used to demonstrate AgenTwin,
    followed by the evaluation methodology that assesses the artifact's
    semantic, functional, and performance characteristics.
The use case provides the concrete operational scenario against which all evaluation
    dimensions are measured, grounding the assessment in a realistic deployment context.

\section{Cross-Domain Use Case Definition}
\label{sec:use-case}

To validate AgenTwin's agent-mediated interoperability approach,
    a realistic cross-domain scenario has been designed that requires
    cooperation between Smart City and Energy Grid DT systems.

The use case addresses the operational challenge of coordinating overnight EV fleet charging
    across facilities managed by two organizationally separate institutions:
    the New York City Department of Citywide Administrative Services (DCAS),
    which operates the city's fleet EV charging network across municipal garages in all five boroughs,
    and Consolidated Edison (ConEdison),
    the distribution utility responsible for the electrical grid serving those facilities.
Depot charging is a centralized EV charging model in which fleet vehicles return to
    a common operating facility for scheduled energy replenishment during non-operational hours,
    enabling predictable vehicle availability and simplified infrastructure
    management.
The challenge intensifies for mixed fleets because power requirements vary substantially
    by vehicle category: light-duty vehicles typically charge at 7--22~kW,
    while heavy-duty vehicles may require over 120~kW,
    making uniform infrastructure planning insufficient.
Moreover, when the combined electrical demand of a fleet approaches a facility's
    service capacity, grid upgrades may be required to prevent overloading
    the distribution infrastructure~\cite{monta2026}.
In an operational context, this means that a safe overnight charging assignment
    cannot be derived from charging station data alone:
    it requires real-time knowledge of transformer headroom held exclusively
    by the serving utility.

\textbf{User Persona:}
A Fleet Coordinator at DCAS is responsible for ensuring that vehicles across several
    city departments are road-ready at morning dispatch.
Each night, as vehicles return to their respective agency facilities across the five boroughs,
    the coordinator must confirm that the combined overnight charging load can be accommodated
    by the local distribution grid at each facility without exceeding transformer capacity.
DCAS operates charging infrastructure for a heterogeneous fleet spanning five agency categories,
    each corresponding to a distinct \texttt{allowedVehicleType} in the NYC Fleet Station Network:
    NYPD patrol and detective sedans (\texttt{car}:
        Chevrolet Bolt EV at 7.2~kW, Hyundai Ioniq~5 at 10.9~kW);
    FDNY Emergency Medical Services paramedic response units (\texttt{car}:
        Hyundai Ioniq~5);
    DSNY refuse collection trucks (\texttt{truck}:
        Mack~LR Electric at up to 150~kW DC);
    DOE electric school buses (\texttt{bus}:
        Thomas Built Buses Jouley at up to 60~kW DC);
    and a NYCEM mobile command vehicle (\texttt{caravan}:
        Mercedes-Benz eSprinter EV at 22~kW)~\cite{dcas2026cftp}.
Power requirements span more than an order of magnitude across these categories,
    making uniform infrastructure planning insufficient.
The coordinator opens AgenTwin and submits a natural-language request:
    \textit{``I need to schedule tonight's depot charge.
    Incoming vehicles: 8 Chevrolet Bolt EVs (NYPD), 4 Hyundai Ioniq~5s (FDNY EMS),
    2 Thomas Built Jouley school buses (DOE), 1 eSprinter mobile command unit (NYCEM),
    and 2 Mack~LR Electric collection trucks (DSNY).
    Which stations can charge all of them without exceeding grid limits?''}

\textbf{Cross-Domain Requirement:}
This request crosses an organizational data sovereignty boundary:
    DCAS and ConEdison are independent institutions operating separate Digital Twin systems
    over the same physical infrastructure,
    with no shared data model:

\begin{itemize}
    \item The \textbf{Smart City DT} (DCAS) knows charging station locations,
        plug counts, operational status, and current slot availability,
        but has no access to ConEdison's transformer loads or grid capacity data
    \item The \textbf{Energy Grid DT} (ConEdison) monitors distribution transformer loads
        and computes available headroom per substation zone,
        but does not model DCAS charging station infrastructure or fleet vehicle assignments
\end{itemize}

AgenTwin bridges these two platforms:
    it correlates charging station locations with ConEdison substation service zones,
    computes available capacity per transformer zone,
    and produces a fleet-wide charging assignment that satisfies both
    station availability and the grid's maximum import constraint.

\textbf{Interaction Workflow:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Operator submits request} via natural language through user interface,
        identifying vehicle models and fleet size.
    \item \textbf{Vehicle enrichment:} the Energy Grid agent resolves each vehicle model against
        \texttt{ElectricVehicle} entities in Ditto,
        retrieving \texttt{chargeRateMax} per vehicle model without requiring
        the operator to provide electrical specifications.
    \item \textbf{Infrastructure query:} the Smart City agent queries \texttt{EVChargingStation}
        entities in KTWIN, filtering by
        \texttt{availableCapacity > 0} and
        \texttt{status = working}.
    \item \textbf{Grid capacity query:} the Energy Grid agent aggregates
        \texttt{CustomerLoad.p} and \texttt{EquivalentLoad.p} values
        per ConEdison substation zone in Ditto,
        computing available headroom as
        $\texttt{headroom} = \texttt{ratedS} - \Sigma(\texttt{CustomerLoad.p} + \texttt{EquivalentLoad.p})$.
    \item \textbf{Geographic--topological bridge:} the agents correlate each candidate station's
        GeoJSON coordinates (\texttt{GeoLocation}) with the CIM \texttt{Location} position points
        of ConEdison substation zones to determine which substation serves each station.
        This step constitutes the primary semantic gap: the two platforms
        represent geographic position in structurally incompatible formats with no shared identifier.
    \item \textbf{Assignment computation:} each vehicle is mapped to a station whose substation zone
        can absorb its \texttt{chargeRateMax} while maintaining at least 5\% safety headroom.
        Vehicles with higher power requirements are preferentially assigned to zones
        with greater available capacity.
    \item \textbf{Assignment proposal presented} to the operator as a structured natural-language
        response listing each vehicle and its assigned charging station.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Evaluation Scenario:}
Both DT platforms are pre-populated with a known city state drawn from real-world sources:
    ConEdison substation locations sourced from OpenStreetMap,
    and DCAS fleet charging station locations from the NYC Open Data portal,
    with transformer electrical parameters synthesized from representative overnight baseline loads.
The evaluation submits the fleet charging request and verifies that
    AgenTwin produces a complete, constraint-satisfying assignment
    without any manually coded mapping between the two platforms.

\textbf{Success Criteria:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item All vehicles in the request receive a valid charging station assignment
    \item No ConEdison transformer zone exceeds 95\% of rated capacity after the full assignment
    \item Only stations with \texttt{status = working} and \texttt{availableCapacity > 0} are assigned
\end{itemize}

    \emph{conceptual} (same physical object modeled from incompatible domain perspectives),
    \emph{structural} (same geographic concept encoded in incompatible data formats), and

\section{Evaluation Methodology}
\label{sec:evaluation-methodology}

The evaluation assesses whether AgenTwin achieves its design goal:
    enabling agent-mediated semantic interoperability between heterogeneous Digital Twin systems
    without requiring manually coded integration between platforms.
Following the DSR evaluation principle of demonstrating artifact feasibility~\cite{peffers2007},
    the scope is deliberately bounded to the fleet charging coordination use case.

The evaluation is structured as three layered dimensions that build on one another.
Semantic mapping evaluation establishes, at the component level, that agents correctly
    bridge each identified semantic gap before any end-to-end claim is made.
Functional evaluation then verifies, end-to-end, that the artifact produces correct
    outputs across both nominal and boundary operating conditions.
Performance evaluation characterises the operational cost of the integration mechanism,
    informing whether the approach is practical for deployment.

\subsection{Semantic Mapping Evaluation}
\label{subsec:semantic-mapping-evaluation}

Semantic mapping evaluation assesses whether agents correctly bridge each semantic gap
    identified in Table~\ref{tab:semantic-gaps}.
To ensure test/production parity, the agent is designed to always emit a
    structured mapping declaration as part of its response.
This artifact names the entities correlated, the reasoning used,
    and any coordinate or encoding transformations applied.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Mapping Coverage:} % Suggestion: use deepEval JSONCorrectnessMetric
    Verify that the structured mapping declaration contains a valid entry for each
        semantic gap in Table~\ref{tab:semantic-gaps}.
    Evaluated via a JSON correctness metric that verifies whether the structured output
        conforms to the expected schema: expected gaps must exist or the test fails.

    \item \textbf{Mapping Correctness:} % Suggestion: use deepEval DAGMetric (BinaryJudgementNode per gap)
    Verify that each entry in the mapping declaration is semantically accurate
        against the specific challenge of its gap.
    Evaluated via a deterministic decision tree that uses LLM-as-a-judge at each
        branching point:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \texttt{EVChargingStation} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{CustomerLoad}
            (Conceptual):
            the agent must have correlated entities by geographic proximity,
            mapping the Smart City infrastructure asset to the Energy Grid load entity.
        \item \texttt{GeoLocation} $\leftrightarrow$ \texttt{Location}
            (Structural):
            the agent must have correctly identified and resolved the structural
            difference between WGS84 GeoJSON and the OGC URN-declared
            coordinate reference system.
        \item \texttt{allowedVehicleType} and \texttt{evName}
            (Vocabulary): 
            the agent must process the User prompt to address two independent problems:
            \begin{itemize}
                \item \emph{Type inference:} the agent must have inferred the
                    \texttt{VehicleType} based on training knowledge
                    to filter compatible \texttt{EVChargingStation}.
                \item \emph{Model identification:} the agent must have matched
                    the correct \texttt{ElectricVehicle}
                    to retrieve \texttt{chargeRateMax} for the 
                    headroom calculation.
            \end{itemize}
    \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Functional Evaluation}
\label{subsec:functional-evaluation}

Functional evaluation verifies that the artifact produces correct outputs
    across two operating points: a nominal scenario where transformer capacity is sufficient
    to accommodate the full fleet, and a stress scenario where it is not.
Together, these two cases confirm that the system satisfies hard constraints
    regardless of whether demand fits within available headroom.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Nominal Scenario:} % Suggestion: use deepEval TaskComplitionMetric + programmatic assertions
    Execute the cross-domain use case described in Section~\ref{sec:use-case}
        with transformer headroom sufficient for the full vehicle fleet.

    \item \textbf{Oversubscription Scenario:} % Suggestion: use deepEval TaskComplitionMetric (strict_mode=True)
    Reduce available transformer headroom so that it cannot accommodate the full fleet.
    Verify that the system aborts the assignment and explains why it could not proceed
        (e.g., which transformer zone would be exceeded).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Performance Evaluation}
\label{subsec:performance-evaluation}

Performance evaluation therefore characterizes AgenTwin's operational overhead
    against the constraints of the deployment context itself,
    and presents the results as an open discussion of practical feasibility.
The goal is to determine whether the approach imposes prohibitive costs
    for realistic operational use,
    with a manually-coded integration baseline identified as a direction for future work.

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Task Completion Time:} % Suggestion: use LangFuse
    Measure total time from operator submission to delivery of the complete assignment proposal,
        including any clarification turns between the agent and the operator.
    Report mean, median, and 95th percentile times across multiple executions.
    Results are discussed against the operational constraint that the assignment must be
        ready before the charging cycle begins to ensure fleet readiness by scheduled
        departure time.

    \item \textbf{LLM Resource Consumption:} % Suggestion: use LangFuse
    Record the number of LLM inference calls and total tokens consumed
        per assignment request.
    These metrics constitute the dominant cost driver in agent-mediated systems
        and determine whether the approach is deployable under realistic budget constraints.
    Results are reported descriptively to inform infrastructure requirements
        and support future cost comparisons as the research matures.
\end{itemize}
